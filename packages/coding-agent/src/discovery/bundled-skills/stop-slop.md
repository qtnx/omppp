# Stop Slop

Remove predictable AI writing patterns while keeping the writer's meaning, facts, voice, and required wording intact.

**Trigger:** Use this skill when drafting, editing, or reviewing prose to remove predictable AI tells.

## Core Rules

1. **Cut filler.** Remove throat-clearing openers, emphasis crutches, business jargon, and unnecessary adverbs. Start with the point. Keep an adverb when removing it changes the fact, tone, or required wording.

2. **Break formulaic structures.** Avoid binary contrasts (`not X, but Y`), negative listings, dramatic fragments, rhetorical setups, false agency, and other predictable templates. State the useful point directly.

3. **Use active voice.** Give each sentence a human subject doing something. Replace passive constructions with an actor and action. Do not let an inanimate object perform a human act (for example, replace “the complaint becomes a fix” with the person who fixes it).

4. **Be specific.** Replace vague declaratives such as “The reasons are structural” or “The implications are significant” with the actual reason or implication. Do not use lazy extremes such as “every,” “always,” or “never” when they hide a narrower claim.

5. **Put the reader in the room.** Prefer “you” or a named person to distant narrator language such as “people” or “users” when the audience is known. Use concrete situations instead of abstractions.

6. **Vary rhythm.** Mix short and long sentences. Break metronomic sequences, vary paragraph endings, and avoid three matching items when two will do. Do not use em dashes; rewrite with a period, comma, colon, or parentheses.

7. **Trust readers.** State facts directly. Remove softening, redundant justification, hand-holding, and meta-joiners such as “the rest of this essay” or “here’s what this means.”

8. **Cut quotables.** Rewrite lines that sound engineered for a pull quote, slogan, or dramatic final punch. Prefer a plain sentence that carries the same information.

## Preserve the Source

Editing for AI tells must not alter meaning or evidence. Preserve all facts, qualifiers, numbers, dates, names, identifiers, URLs, code, quoted text, data, citations, and required legal or technical wording exactly unless the user explicitly asks for a content change. Do not invent specificity, remove a necessary qualification, or change a claim merely to make prose sound more human.

## Quick Checks Before Delivery

- Remove unnecessary adverbs and throat-clearing openers.
- Find passive voice and name the actor.
- Replace inanimate subjects doing human work with the responsible person or team.
- Restructure sentences that start with a Wh-word when the opening feels formulaic.
- Cut “here’s what,” “this,” or “that” as empty setup when the sentence can begin with the point.
- State the positive claim directly instead of using a “not X, it’s Y” contrast.
- Break three consecutive sentences with the same length or cadence.
- Vary a paragraph that ends in a punchy one-liner.
- Remove every em dash and preserve the surrounding meaning.
- Replace vague declaratives with named facts or implications.
- Put narrator-from-a-distance claims such as “Nobody designed this” into the reader's concrete scene.
- Delete meta-commentary and meta-joiners.
- Check that code, quotes, data, identifiers, and required legal or technical wording remain exact.

## Before and After

**Filler and vague claim**

- Before: “Here’s what this means: the implications are significant.”
- After: “The change raises support costs.”

**Passive voice and abstraction**

- Before: “The report was reviewed, and improvements were made.”
- After: “Maya reviewed the report and fixed the broken links.”

**Formulaic contrast and quotable rhythm**

- Before: “It’s not about moving faster. It’s about moving smarter. That is the future of work.”
- After: “The team should remove the approval step that delays releases.”

The rewrites above change style, not the underlying claim. Apply the same restraint to real text: if a concrete detail is not present in the source, do not add one.

## Scoring

Rate the draft from 1 to 10 on each dimension:

| Dimension | Question |
|---|---|
| Directness | Does it make statements instead of announcements? |
| Rhythm | Does the cadence vary rather than sound metronomic? |
| Trust | Does it respect the reader's intelligence? |
| Authenticity | Does it sound like a person rather than a template? |
| Density | Is every remaining sentence earning its place? |

A maximum score is 50. **Below 35/50, revise before delivery.**
